Races, diplomacy and events

Who the empires are, what they are like and what they say: race templates, traits, personalities, encounters and events.

families: 6files: 152 tags: 258generated from: ef406fa04

A race file is also the empire's startup template: every Default*, Pirate* and Remnant* line is a ship-design name, so changing one changes what that empire starts with and what its governors build. Traits are chosen at game start and add their modifiers to the empire. Diplomacy personalities set starting relations and how fast they drift, while dialog pools only choose the words. Encounters are looked up by faction and step, and exploration events by their own name.

Level 3 — how this section works

A missing design name falls back to a default, often a vanilla one, so a typo looks like the AI ignoring your ship rather than an error. Trait Excludes is a name and trait points come from Globals.TraitPoints. An encounter is selected by Faction plus Step, and the first loaded match wins, so two files with the same pair shadow each other. Event outcomes are weighted by Chance and can spawn ships, grant a tech or edit the planet. A missing dialog pool falls back to Default and never blocks the treaty.

Identity 15 tags
Who the race is.

These names appear throughout the UI and the save.

Level 3 — the detail

The traits name is the race UID; the portrait drives the flag and colour.

  • Traitselement The container the loader turns into EmpireData.Traits.
    LoadEmpires deserializes this block into the RacialTrait that almost every racial stat is read from.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `EmpireData` has no fields of its own for race identity: `Name`, `Singular`, `ShipType` and the rest are pass-throughs to `Traits`. After loading, `LoadEmpires` repairs an empty `ShipType` from `Singular` with a warning, and `FixSpacePortScale` corrects a scale of zero or less. A tag written outside this block that also exists inside it is dropped, which is why the race files nest everything here.

  • Namestring The race id, and the name the game shows for the empire.
    `EmpireData.Name` returns `Traits.Name`, and the save, `Universe.GetEmpireByName` and the encounter lookup all compare against that string.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `EmpireData.Name` is a pass-through to `Traits.Name`, so this is the id, not only a label. The save stores empires by it, `Universe.GetEmpireByName` finds them by it, and `ResourceManager.GetEncounter` matches an encounter's `Faction` against it. Renaming it strands saved empires and any encounter written for the old string; `Singular` and `Plural` are separate and do not follow.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <Name>Cordrazine Collective</Name>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • Singularstring One member of the race, used wherever a sentence talks about a single person.
    It fills the `SING` token in story encounters, is the subject of the race-description paragraphs, labels the owner column in the planet and exotic-system lists, and appears in invasion alerts such as 'Human troops are landing'. It also becomes the race's `ShipType` when that field is left empty, so it decides the default hull family.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `EncounterInstance.FormatText` replaces `SING` with it, `SolarSystemBody.OwnerName` and `PlanetListScreenItem` print it, and `ResourceManager` copies it into `ShipType` when that field is blank. The race setup screen searches for the race whose `Singular` is exactly `Human` to choose its default selection, so renaming that value silently opens setup on the first major race instead. Empty text is accepted and produces blank subjects plus an empty `ShipType`, which then warns at load and strips that race's hull and dialogue keys. It is stored in the save, so a change reaches new games only.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <Singular>Cordrazine</Singular>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • Pluralstring Many members of the race, used where the sentence treats them as a people.
    The race-description builder inserts it before phrases like 'are natural sailors', the budget screen lists trade partners by it, and the espionage screen names the target people with it. Rebel factions replace it with `RebelPlur`, so a rebellion does not inherit the parent's name.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `RaceDesignScreen_RaceDescription` calls `.Plural(...)` on the generated paragraphs, which makes this the word the setup screen repeats most. `BudgetScreen` prints it in the trade list and `AgentListItem` in the espionage target list, while notifications use `Singular` instead, which is why both fields are needed. `CreateRebelsFromEmpireData` overwrites it with `RebelPlur` and `Singular` with `RebelSing` at rebellion time. There is no fallback and no localisation id, so an empty value leaves a gap in every one of those sentences.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <Plural>Cordrazine</Plural>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • Adj1string An adjective for the race, substituted into story text.
    Only the encounter formatter reads it: an `ADJ1` token in an event's text becomes this word. The race setup screen passes the value through unchanged and offers no field to edit it, so a custom race keeps the adjective of the file it was based on.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `EncounterInstance.FormatText` handles the `ADJ1` and `ADJ2` tokens and no other code reads either field. Vanilla sets `strange` in three race files and leaves the rest empty. The word is inserted as written with no capitalisation fix, so an event that opens a sentence with the token needs a capitalised value. An empty value substitutes nothing and leaves the event's sentence short rather than failing.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <Adj1>strange</Adj1>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • Adj2string A second adjective for the race, used by the same event text as `Adj1`.
    The encounter formatter substitutes it for an `ADJ2` token. Like `Adj1` it has no screen of its own and does not appear in the race description, so it only shows up where a hand-written event asks for it.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `EncounterInstance.FormatText` is the only reader, through the `ADJ2` case next to `ADJ1`. Vanilla sets `eerie` in the same three race files that set `strange`. The value is used verbatim, so capitalisation and punctuation come from the event, not from the field. A missing value produces an empty substitution with no warning.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <Adj2>eerie</Adj2>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • HomeworldNamestring The name of the race's home planet, as shown in text.
    It is a label, not a rename: the starting planet keeps whatever name its solar system file gives it, and this string is what the interface calls that world. It appears in the diplomacy intel line, the closing sentence of the race description and the espionage dossier's home-planet field.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `MainDiplomacyScreen` prints it under the Homeworld heading and `RaceDesignScreen_RaceDescription` ends with 'The <Singular> homeworld of <value>'. `DossierPanel` copies it into the agent's `HomePlanet` label, which is the only place espionage shows it. Galaxy generation never reads it, so changing it on an existing race changes text and nothing else, and a value that disagrees with the planet's real name is not an error. The human file and the three factions all name Earth.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — <HomeworldName>Earth</HomeworldName>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • HomeSystemNamestring The file stem of the solar system where the empire starts.
    Galaxy generation loads `SolarSystems/<value>.xml` for the empire's starting system, and that file supplies the homeworld the race begins on. If no file matches, the game generates a random system under the same name instead, so a typo costs the hand-built layout but not the start.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `CreateSystemPlaceHolders` calls `LoadSolarSystemData(HomeSystemName)` and keeps a null when the file is missing. `GenerateSystems` then takes the null branch and calls `GenerateRandomSystem` with the same name, while a non-null result goes through `GenerateFromData` and uses the file's planets, resources and placement. Several vanilla races share `Sol`, and two races naming the same system each get their own copy of its layout rather than sharing one. The field is read only when a galaxy is created, so a save keeps the system it was generated with.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — <HomeSystemName>Sol</HomeSystemName>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • PortraitNamestring The race's portrait id and the key its per-race AI settings are filed under.
    The game loads `Portraits/<value>` for the race's picture in diplomacy, espionage, the governor panel and the relations diagram, and uses the same string to find the race's block in `Research.yaml` and `Budgets.yaml`. It also names the Markov name file that labels planets in that empire's new systems.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `ResourceManager.Texture("Portraits/" + PortraitName)` draws the picture everywhere it appears, and a missing texture logs and falls back to the red `NewUI/x_red` placeholder rather than failing. `ResearchOptions` matches its per-race block on this string or `All`, while `BudgetPriorities` reads only the `All` block and warns that every other block is ignored. `GetRandomNames` builds the path `NameGenerators/spynames_<value>.txt` and returns no generator when that file is absent, so planet naming in new systems quietly loses its custom names. `FindRebellion` compares this value against a rebel name, so two races sharing a value are treated as the same rebellion, which is why the three faction files reuse `unknown`. Vanilla uses the short name here even when `Name` is a full title (`Human`, not `The United Federation`).

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — <PortraitName>unknown</PortraitName>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • FlagIndexint Zero-based index of the sprite in the `Flags` atlas that marks this empire.
    The flag is drawn on diplomacy cards, the relations diagram, colony and mining overlays, event popups and the espionage list, tinted with the empire colour. `0` is the first flag in the atlas, not 'no flag'; only a save header uses `-1` to mean that.

    Allowed range: 0 to the flag count minus one; the setup screen clamps to that, a race file is not clamped. Vanilla runs from 3 (Human) to 64 (Corsairs and Unknown), and the count follows the `Flags` atlas.

    Level 3 — the detail

    `ResourceManager.Flag(index)` returns the sprite or null past the end of the atlas, and the draw helpers dereference it, so an out-of-range value throws in the first screen that draws the flag instead of showing a blank. `RaceDesignScreen` wraps its picker between 0 and `NumFlags - 1` and `SavedGame` stores the player's flag by index with `-1` for absent, but nothing corrects a race file. The value is cosmetic only: it changes which sprite marks the empire and nothing about its relations or strength.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — <FlagIndex>64</FlagIndex>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • R/G/Bfloat The three 8-bit channels of the empire colour.
    `RacialTrait.Color` casts each channel to a byte, and the flag, border and map tints all use the resulting colour.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The cast is unchecked, so a value above 255 or below zero wraps instead of clamping. The three tags are read together into one `Color`, so a race that sets only one channel gets a strongly tinted empire rather than a neutral one. The new-game ship preview and the border drawing use the same colour.

  • ShipTypestring The race's ship-style id: the string that ties it to hulls, tech unlocks and its own text and name files.
    Hulls carry a matching `Style`, and a tech's `<HullsUnlocked><ShipType>` applies only when it is empty, `ALL`, or this value, so the same tree gives each people a different hull list. The same string also picks the race's diplomacy lines, spy-name file, vanity-name pool and role labels, and the setup screen uses it to find the race's archetype.

    Allowed range: any string, but it must match the `Style` of the hulls you want and the keys already used by the dialogue, spy-name and ship-name files. Vanilla has 13 values: Terran for Human, plus Cordrazine, Pollops, Opteris, Draylok, Kulrathi, Vulfen, Ralyeh, Revoran, Draug, Corsair, Remnant and Unknown.

    Level 3 — the detail

    `WeCanBuildThis` refuses a design until its hull is in `UnlockedHullsDict`, which `TechEntry.UnLockHulls` fills only from tech entries whose `<ShipType>` passes `IsTypeUnlockableBy` — empty, `ALL`, or this value — so this is the field that makes a hull tech racial. The restriction is not permanent ownership: `CheckSource` also accepts a tech stolen from or conquered from that people, and `ShipStyleMatch` keeps an absorbed empire's styles buildable, which is how a race can end up flying another people's hulls. The same string drives UI and spawns: the design browser sorts the race's own style first, the race setup preview picks its largest cruiser, and scenario and faction spawns filter hulls by it. It also keys text and names — `Opinion_Positive_/Neutral_/Negative_<ShipType>` in the diplomacy file, `spynames_<ShipType>.txt` for agents (falling back to Humans), vanity names in `ShipNames/ShipNames.xml`, and race-specific role labels in `ShipRoles`. An empty value is replaced at load by the race's `Singular` with only a warning, after which its hull entries, dialogue keys and name files no longer match; a wrong value silently gives a race with a thinner hull list and blank opinion lines.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <ShipType>Cordrazine</ShipType>; the other 12 files set their own.

  • VideoPathstring The empire's intro video, and the stem of its portrait.
    `ScreenMediaPlayer` plays it as the empire's video, and `RacialTrait.Icon` loads `Portraits/<VideoPath>`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `PlayVideo` is called with `empire.data.Traits.VideoPath`, and `RacialTrait.Icon` uses the same string as a portrait file name, falling back to `Portraits/Unknown`. One value therefore drives two assets, so a missing video and a missing portrait fail together. The string is a file stem, not a path with an extension.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <VideoPath>Cordrazine</VideoPath>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • MusicCuestring The track this empire plays in peace.
    `ScreenMediaPlayer` plays it while the empire is selected and not at war, and swaps to `CombatMusic` during war.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The cue is chosen in `ScreenMediaPlayer` and only when nothing is already playing, so it is the ambient theme rather than a jingle. A name that is not a loaded cue leaves the previous track playing, and the value is an audio file stem under `Audio`. War replaces it entirely, which is why a race that sets no cue still has combat music.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <MusicCue>Colomn_03_loop</MusicCue>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DiplomacyDialogPathstring The file stem of the diplomacy lines this race speaks.
    It names a file, not a path: the game looks for `DiplomacyDialogs/<Language>/<value>.xml` and reads its dialog sets for greetings, treaty exchanges and opinion lines. A mod file with the same name inherits every line it omits from the vanilla file, so only the changed lines need shipping.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `LoadDialogs` indexes the files by stem and `GetDiplomacyDialog` is a map lookup that throws `MapKeyNotFoundException` when the key is missing. Empire creation calls it unconditionally in `CreateEmpireFromEmpireData`, so a wrong value is fatal at game start, before the diplomacy screen is ever opened. `SharedDiplomacy` is loaded separately by that screen for generic lines and is not a fallback for this field. Vanilla gives the three faction files `Human`, because they reuse the human lines rather than shipping their own.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — <DiplomacyDialogPath>Human</DiplomacyDialogPath>; the other 12 files set their own.

Starting ships and defaults 13 tags
The design the AI builds for each job.

All values are design names; the AI falls back when one is missing.

Level 3 — the detail

Changing these changes what the empire starts with and what its governors build.

  • StartingShipstring A design name the load validates for the empire.
    `CheckForRequiredShipDesigns` resolves it and logs an error for a major race when no design carries that name, and `CarrierBays` returns it as a fallback fighter name.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The assert runs once at content load, so a typo is a log line rather than a crash, and `IsFactionOrMinorRace` exempts factions from the complaint. The only other reader is `CarrierBays`, which falls back to it when a hull asks for a default fighter and names none. It is a design `Name`, not a file path.

    In vanilla: Races/Remnant.xml — <StartingShip>Xeno Fighter</StartingShip>; the other 11 files set their own.

  • StartingScoutstring The scout design the empire falls back to.
    `EmpireData.ScoutShip` returns the automation pick, then this field, then the literal `Unarmed Scout`, and the load asserts the resolved name.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The precedence lives in `ScoutShip`, so a player who picks a scout in the automation window overrides this field without editing the race. An empty value still produces a usable scout, but it is the generic vanilla design rather than one matched to the race. The assert checks the resolved name, not the tag alone.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <StartingScout>Vingscout</StartingScout>; the other 8 files set their own.

  • PrototypeShipstring The design name that pays no upkeep.
    `ShipMaintenance.IsFreeUpkeepShip` returns true for any ship whose name matches, and the load asserts the design exists.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The check has an overload for a live `Ship` and one for an `IShipDesign`, so the prototype and everything built to it are free. That makes the field a cost lever as well as a name, since pointing it at an expensive design removes that design's maintenance. Remnant empires are free regardless of the setting.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <PrototypeShip>Cordrazine-Prototype</PrototypeShip>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultColonyShipstring The colony ship the empire builds by default.
    `EmpireData.ColonyShip` returns the automation pick, then this field, then `Colony Ship`, and the load asserts it is a colony ship.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The fallback chain means a race that names nothing still expands, using the vanilla design. The assert checks `IsColonyShip`, so a freighter here logs an error and the resolved name is still what the AI tries to build. The automation window lists the valid designs and writes the player's pick to `CurrentAutoColony`, which outranks this field.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultColonyShip>Cordrazine Colonizer</DefaultColonyShip>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultSmallTransportstring The freighter the empire builds by default.
    `EmpireData.FreighterShip` returns the automation pick, then this field, then `Small Transport`, and the load asserts both `IsFreighter` and `IsCandidateForTradingBuild`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    Two asserts run on this one tag, so a design that can carry cargo but is not a trading candidate still logs an error. The `FreighterShip` fallback is what the AI uses when auto-freighters are left alone. The automation window exposes the same slot and stores the player's choice in `CurrentAutoFreighter`.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultSmallTransport>Owlwok Freighter S</DefaultSmallTransport>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultTroopShipstring The troop transport the empire builds by default.
    `Empire.CommonInitialize` fills an empty value with `<PortraitName> Troop`, and the load asserts the result is a single-troop ship.
    Level 3 — the detail

    Because the default is derived from `PortraitName`, a race that leaves the tag out looks for a design named after its portrait, which usually does not exist. `IsSingleTroopShip` is the shape the assert wants, so a multi-troop design here logs an error. Invasion goals ask the empire for its troop ship by this resolved name.

    In vanilla: Races/Human.xml — <DefaultTroopShip>Human Troop</DefaultTroopShip>; the other 8 files set their own.

  • DefaultConstructorstring The constructor the empire builds by default.
    `EmpireData.ConstructorShip` returns the automation pick, then this field, then `Terran Constructor`, and the load asserts `IsConstructor`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The literal fallback is a Terran design, so a race that names nothing here builds a ship from another people's tree. The assert is what catches a name that resolves to something that cannot build. The automation window stores the player's choice in `CurrentConstructor`, which outranks the tag.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultConstructor>Owlwok Constructor</DefaultConstructor>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultMiningShipstring The mining ship the empire builds by default.
    `Empire.GetMiningShipName` returns it when the design exists and otherwise `DefaultMiningShipName`, and the load asserts `BaseCargoSpace > 0`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The test is `ResourceManager.ShipTemplateExists`, so an empty or misspelt name silently falls back to the shared default. The cargo assert is the only check that the design can actually mine. Mining goals and the automation window both ask for the name through the same method.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultMiningShip>Owlwok Mining Ship</DefaultMiningShip>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultResearchStationstring The research station the empire builds by default.
    `EmpireData.ResearchStation` returns the automation pick, then this field, then `Basic Research Station`, and the load asserts `IsResearchStation`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The assert checks the role, not the name, so a station with the wrong role logs an error while the fallback still gives the AI something to build. Research goals ask for the resolved name, which means a race with no tag uses the vanilla station. The automation window writes the player's choice to `CurrentResearchStation`.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultResearchStation>Basic Research Station</DefaultResearchStation>; all 8 files set it the same way.

  • DefaultMiningStationstring The mining station the empire builds by default.
    `EmpireData.MiningStation` returns the automation pick, then this field, then `Basic Mining Station`, and the load asserts `IsMiningStation`.
    Level 3 — the detail

    As with the research station, the fallback keeps the AI working when the tag is absent and the assert only complains about the role. The station is built on a planet's mining slot, so the role is what makes it legal there. `CurrentMiningStation` outranks the tag once a player picks one.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultMiningStation>Cordrazine Basic Mining Station</DefaultMiningStation>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultSalvageShipstring The salvage ship the empire builds when salvage ops are on.
    `SalvageShipDesignOf` returns it only when the design exists and `IsSalvageShip`, and the load warns when salvage ops are enabled and the race names none.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The warning appears in `CheckForRequiredShipDesigns` only when `GlobalStats.Defaults.EnableSalvageOps` is set, so a mod that leaves the feature off never needs the tag. The automation window offers the same slot through `CurrentSalvageShip`. A design that exists but is not a salvage ship is treated as none.

    In vanilla: Races/Cordrazine.xml — <DefaultSalvageShip>Cordrazine Salvage Ship</DefaultSalvageShip>; the other 7 files set their own.

  • DefaultAssaultShuttlestring The shuttle used for boarding and troop landings.
    `Empire.GetAssaultShuttleName` returns it when the design exists, otherwise `BoardingShuttle.Name`, and troop bays launch with that name.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The existence test is `ResourceManager.ShipTemplateExists`, so an empty value falls back to the shared boarding shuttle rather than failing. `ShipModule` sets a troop bay's `HangarShipUID` from this method, and `Ship_Troop` creates assault ships with it. It is a design name, and the assert checks only that it resolves.

    In vanilla: Races/Human.xml — <DefaultAssaultShuttle>Terran Assault Shuttle</DefaultAssaultShuttle>; the other 8 files set their own.

  • DefaultSupplyShuttlestring The shuttle that ferries ordnance and supplies.
    `Empire.GetSupplyShuttleName` returns it when the design exists, otherwise `DefaultSupplyShuttleName`, and supply bays and refund goals ask for it by that method.
    Level 3 — the detail

    Supply bays, `DeliverRefund`, `RearmShipFromPlannet` and planet building all call the method, so one tag feeds every supply route the empire has. The fallback keeps those routes working when the tag is absent or misspelt. The load asserts `IsSupplyShuttle` on the value, which catches a freighter parked in the wrong slot.

    In vanilla: Races/Human.xml — <DefaultSupplyShuttle>Terran-Supply</DefaultSupplyShuttle>; the other 8 files set their own.

Pirates and remnants 39 tags
The designs those factions use.

Only read for pirate or remnant factions.

Level 3 — the detail

A missing entry falls back to a default design name.

  • IsPirateFactionbool Marks the race as a pirate faction.
    `EmpireAI` gives the empire the pirate persona when this flag and `IsFaction` are both set, which is what starts tribute and raid goals.
    Level 3 — the detail

    A race with the flag but no `Faction` value is an ordinary empire that never gets the pirate AI. The pair is read once when the empire is created, so editing the tag mid-game changes nothing. The persona is what loads `Pirates` and its base, raid and payment goals.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — 1 entry: true; Races/Draugar.xml — 1 entry: true.

  • IsRemnantFactionbool Marks the race as a remnant faction.
    `EmpireAI` gives the empire the remnant persona when this flag and `IsFaction` are both set.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `Remnants` then owns the empire's goals, ship costs and guardian behaviour, and every ship it spawns is marked `IsGuardian`. As with pirates, the flag does nothing without a non-zero `Faction`. The persona is chosen at creation, so a save keeps the one it was made with.

    In vanilla: Races/Remnant.xml — 1 entry: true.

  • PirateBaseBasicstring The basic pirate base design.
    `PirateForces` uses it while the effective level is 4 or less, and `GetBaseNames` counts it as a base for defence.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The effective level is the faction level times a difficulty multiplier (0.7 on normal, 0.8 on hard), so easier games stay on this tier longer. `GetBaseNames` returns all three tiers, which is how `IsBase` recognises a base whatever built it. A missing design leaves that tier with no base.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — 1 entry: Corsair Asteroid Base; Races/Draugar.xml — 1 entry: Draug Asteroid Base.

  • PirateFighterBasicstring The basic pirate fighter design.
    `PirateForces` uses it up to effective level 4 and `AddToShipWeCanSpawn` lists it for raids.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `Pirates.AdvanceInShips` adds the improved fighter at level 5 and the advanced at 8, so this design is the starting wing and then a fallback. The fighter is also what a pirate carrier launches, which is why it must be a fighter-role design. An empty value leaves the faction unable to field fighters.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — 1 entry: Corsair-Fighter; Races/Draugar.xml — 1 entry: Draug-Fighter.

  • PirateFrigateBasicstring The basic pirate frigate design.
    `PirateForces` uses it up to effective level 4, and `AddToShipWeCanSpawn` lists it as a raid ship.
    Level 3 — the detail

    The frigate is the pirate line ship, so this design is what a young faction fights with. `AdvanceInShips` replaces it at level 7 with the improved frigate and at 10 with the advanced. An empty value leaves raiding parties without a line ship.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — 1 entry: Corsair; Races/Draugar.xml — 1 entry: Draug.

  • PirateSlaverBasicstring The basic pirate boarding-ship design.
    `PirateForces` uses it as the boarding ship up to effective level 4, and `AddToShipWeCanSpawn` lists it for raids.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `PirateForces` names the field `BoardingShip`, which is what the AI uses when it wants to capture rather than destroy. `AdvanceInShips` upgrades it at level 6 and again at 9. A faction with no slaver can still raid, but it cannot take ships.

    In vanilla: Races/Corsairs.xml — 1 entry: Corsair-Slaver; Races/Draugar.xml — 1 entry: Draug-Slaver.

  • PirateStationBasicstring The basic pirate orbital station design.
    `PirateForces` uses it while the effective level is 4 or less, and `GetStationNames` counts it as a station for defence.

What the people are like.

Weights choose a personality; the trait set chooses starting bonuses.

Level 3 — the detail

TraitSet names a set in RacialTraits.xml; TraitOptions are the options offered.

  • TraitSetstring One trait set inside the list.
    The loader maps it to a `TraitSet` object whose only field is the `TraitOptions` list.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `TraitSets` is an array of these, and the array order matters: index 0 is what a human player is offered and the rest are drawn for the AI. Moving a set therefore changes which traits a player sees. The element carries nothing else, so all of a set's meaning is in its options.

  • TraitSetselement The list of trait sets a race can start with.
    `RacialTrait.TraitSets` is filled from this block, and `LoadTraitConstraints` gives the player set 0 and the AI a random set.
    Level 3 — the detail

    `PlayerTraitOptions` returns `TraitSets[0].TraitOptions`, and the AI path uses `random.Item(TraitSets)`, which is why the vanilla files comment that the first set is the player's. Each option name is matched against `RacialTraits.xml` and its modifiers are added to the race's traits; a name that matches nothing is skipped silently. A race with no sets gets no traits at all.

  • TraitOptionsstring[] The trait names that make up one set.
    `LoadTraitConstraints` adds each named option's modifiers to the race, and an unknown name is ignored without a warning.
    Level 3 — the detail

    Each string must be a `TraitName` in `RacialTraits.xml`; the loop tests `Contains` against the loaded list, so a repeated name changes nothing. The options are added on top of the race's own fields, which is how a trait and a race bonus of the same name stack. What the player sees is the localised name of each option, not the string in the file.

  • PersonalityTraitsWeightsstring[] The pool the empire's diplomatic personality is drawn from.
    `UniverseState_Empires` picks one name per personality trait with `empire.Random.Item`, so a repeated name is weighted.
    Level 3 — the detail

    Each name must be a `DTrait.Name` in `Diplomacy/DiplomaticTraits.xml`; an unknown string still consumes a draw and then contributes nothing. The draw fills `DiplomaticPersonality`, which sets war, peace and treaty behaviour. Listing a name three times is how a race is made reliably aggressive without forcing it.

Growth 6 tags
How fast a colony grows and what it eats.

The six fields are separate: two set growth bounds, one scales the result, one sets the food charge, and two shape the homeworld.

Level 3 — the detail

Population is held in thousandths of a billion, so 0.005 is 5 million people per turn.

  • ConsumptionModifierfloat Scales how much food each billion colonists eats per turn.
    The game charges 1 food per billion colonists per turn before this field, and the value is added to that 1, so -0.15 charges 0.85 and 0.1 charges 1.1. Cybernetic races pay the same charge in production instead of food. It applies to every colony, and to the food that troops in space consume, not only the homeworld.

    Allowed range: the loader sets no bounds; -1 is the lowest sensible value because 1 + value reaches 0, and below -1 the charge turns negative. Math: consumption = 1 + value, per billion colonists per turn; -1 = 0, -0.15 = 0.85, 0 = 1, 0.1 = 1.1, 0.5 = 1.5.

    Level 3 — the detail

    `ConsumptionPerColonist = 1 + ConsumptionModifier` is multiplied by population in billions, so -0.15 saves 0.15 food per billion people per turn and 0.1 adds the same. Vanilla Efficient Metabolism sets -0.15 for 3 points and Gluttonous sets 0.1 and refunds 2. The code applies no clamp: at -1 a colony eats nothing, and below -1 the charge is negative, so keep the value above -1. The tech bonus Consumption Bonus subtracts its value from this field, which is why a positive tech bonus makes food cheaper.

    In vanilla: RacialTraits/RacialTraits.xml, one value per trait option — Efficient Metabolism -0.15 = 0.85 food per billion colonists, Gluttonous 0.1 = 1.1 food per billion colonists.

  • PopGrowthMinfloat Sets a floor on how many people a colony gains each turn.
    Growth is computed from population, spare capacity and the empire's base reproductive rate, and this field raises the result to a minimum. The unit is billions per turn, so 0.005 means at least 5 million people per turn. The floor is applied after the growth ceiling and before ReproductionMod, and it is skipped while a colony is starving, over capacity or in recent combat. Vanilla Fertile sets 0.005.

    Allowed range: the loader sets no bounds; the colony is clamped to 0..MaxPopulation and then to at least 10 people, so a negative floor can shrink a colony but not empty it. Math: floor = value × 1000 people per turn; -0.005 = -5 million, 0 = no floor, 0.005 = 5 million, 0.05 = 50 million.

    Level 3 — the detail

    `repRate = repRate.LowerBound(PopGrowthMin * 1000)` runs on the internal scale where 1000 is one billion, so 0.005 is a floor of 5 units, or 5 million people, per turn. On a small or crowded colony the floor can supply most of the growth, which is why Fertile matters most early. Because the floor applies even when natural growth is negative, a positive value can keep a shrinking colony from emptying. Vanilla uses it only on Fertile, at 0.005 for 2 points.

    In vanilla: RacialTraits/RacialTraits.xml, one value on one trait option — Fertile 0.005 = at least 5 million people per turn, for 2 points.

    Allowed range: the loader sets no bounds; 0 turns the cap off and a negative cap forces population loss. Math: cap = value × 1000 people per turn; -0.005 = at most -5 million, 0 = no cap, 0.015 = at most 15 million, 0.05 = at most 50 million.

    Level 3 — the detail

    `if (PopGrowthMax.NotZero()) repRate = repRate.UpperBound(PopGrowthMax * 1000)` caps the per-turn gain at 0.015 billion, or 15 million people, on the internal scale. It is a ceiling rather than a multiplier, so a world below it grows exactly as it would without the trait. Vanilla Less Fertile sets 0.015 and refunds 3 points, and no other trait sets it.     In vanilla: RacialTraits/RacialTraits.xml, one value on one trait option — Less Fertile 0.015 = at most 15 million people per turn, for -3 points (a refund of 3).
