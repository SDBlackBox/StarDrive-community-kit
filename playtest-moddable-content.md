# Playtest: the moddable content guide

A veteran player's review of `moddable-content.html` and the seven section pages, followed by the
author's response and the changes made because of it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Reviewed | hub + 7 section pages, 45 families, 1,065 tag cards, 5,735 content files |
| Build | `StarDrive@ef406fa`, generated by `docs/kit/build-moddable.py` |
| Tester | 400+ hours in StarDrive, first mod, **no source access** |
| Method | rendered HTML only, plus the content values the pages quote; no C#, loaders or commit history |
| Skill | `.claude/skills/playtest-review/SKILL.md` (reusable tester + author pass) |

The tester read every family, every card and every quoted value. The code was opened only after the
review was written, in the author half. The numbers below are the generator's own audit counts, run
before and after the changes.

## Headline

**The guide is a good map and a thin manual.** At family level it tells a modder what a file is, how
it is loaded, what wins on override and what breaks silently; that part is better than the game's own
documentation. At field level, most cards do not yet answer the questions a modder has when editing a
number.

| measure | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| cards with a real prose level 3 | 84 / 1,065 (7.9%) | 95 / 1,065 (8.9%) |
| cards with a level-3 *heading* but only a restated value | 32 | 0 (marked as index) |
| cards with no level 3 at all | 949 | 970 marked `values only` |
| cards whose level 2 is under two sentences | 1,057 / 1,065 | 1,049 / 1,065 |
| numeric tags with no Allowed-range line | 517 | 511 |
| vanilla lines collapsed to one file + a count | 260 (XML only) | 329 (all formats) |

The honest one-line verdict: **I can find the file and the tag, but for most fields I cannot tell
what a safe value is without opening the source.** The guide now says that on the card instead of
implying otherwise.

## Scorecard by section

| section | cards | real level 3 | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| General game options | 99 | 26 | Partial. The curated globals (maintenance, turn timer, gravity, traits) are the best cards in the document; the opponent/start cards were stubs. |
| Map creation | 85 | 3 | Fail. Planet types, sun zones and spawn weights are the numbers a map mod edits first; almost none have a range or unit. |
| Ships | 264 | 35 | Partial. Modules are the strongest family; hulls and weapons are mostly index cards. |
| Tech tree | 88 | 6 | Fail. Cost is documented; UID, MaxLevel and unlock containers were stubs. |
| Races and diplomacy | 258 | 10 | Fail by count, pass by quality where it exists: the six growth-trait cards are the model the rest should follow. |
| Colonies | 132 | 5 | Fail. Building `Name`, `Cost` and most edict fields are index cards. |
| UI and assets | 139 | 10 | Pass at file level (replace by path, missing texture degrades), fail for UI layout ids (3/52). |

## What worked

**`StartingScout` is the card to copy.** It quotes one race, in the file's own syntax, and counts
the rest:

> In vanilla: `Races/Cordrazine.xml` — `<StartingScout>Vingscout</StartingScout>`; the other 8 files
> set their own.

I know the exact line to edit, that the value is a design name, and that the other eight races are
independent. One line, no list of nine near-identical files.

**`ShipMaintenanceMultiplier` answered a balance question in one card.** It gave the unit (upkeep =
build cost × value), the play consequence (a 1,000-credit frigate costs 5,000/turn at 5), the
interaction (racial MaintMod applies first), and the save caveat. I did not need to test to know
what 5 would do.

**The growth traits show the bar.** `ConsumptionModifier` gives the formula (`1 + value`), the range
with the math at each bound (-1 = 0, -0.15 = 0.85, 0.5 = 1.5), the vanilla traits and their point
cost, and the failure symptom (below -1 the charge turns negative). That is a field I could edit
without fear.

## Findings

### F1 — Blocker: 970 cards are an index, not an explanation

The largest families are mostly a name, a type, one sentence and a list of values. `Buildings/Name`
before this pass:

> **Name** string — Building UID and display name.
> (no level 2, no level 3)
> In vanilla: ... 52 more files.

I cannot tell from that whether renaming it changes the display string, breaks the tech that unlocks
it, or both. The same shape appears on `Races/Name`, `Races/Traits`, `Weapons/Thrust`, and most of
`Research`, `Suns`, `PlanetTypes` and `Buildings/Cost`.

*Fix:* a card with no documented meaning must not look finished. It should say what is missing, and
the reader should not infer a bound from the values shown.

### F2 — Blocker: level 2 is one sentence on 1,049 cards

The contract says level 2 is several sentences on what reads the value and how it combines. In
practice it is a subtitle: `MaxOpponents` — "Hard cap on the dropdown."; `Thrust` — "Sublight
acceleration."; `Weapons/UID` — "Keep equal to the file stem." A modder opening the card learns
nothing that was not in the collapsed line.

*Fix:* write level 2 as the paragraph that explains the mechanism, and reserve the one-line summary
for level 1. This pass added it to 11 cards; the rest remain one sentence.

### F3 — Major: a restated value was presented as level 3

`MaxOpponents` before:

> Level 3 — the detail
> Vanilla 7.
> In vanilla: `Globals.yaml` — 1 entry: 7.

The expander promised detail and delivered the same 7 twice. I counted 32 cards like this ("1.",
"Vanilla 8.", "1.4 default."). They are worse than an empty level 3 because they look answered.

*Fix:* detect and drop value-only level 3s; mark the card instead.

### F4 — Major: the one-file example only ran for XML

`HullName` before:

> In vanilla: `Hulls/.../Cordrazine_Station.hull` — 1 entry: Cordrazine/Cordrazine_Station;
> `.../Dodaving.hull` — 1 entry: Cordrazine/Dodaving; ... 122 more files.

`.hull`, `.design` and YAML cards still dumped the near-identical list, even though the same rule
applies: each hull has one `HullName`. Twenty-five lines of hull paths tell me nothing the first
line does not.

*Fix:* apply the one-example rule to every format and quote the line in that format's syntax.

### F5 — Major: a tech's own UID and a child UID look alike

`Technology/UID` before:

> In vanilla: ... `AdvancedKineticTurrets.xml` — 2 entries: AdvancedKineticTurrets, MagneticTurrets;
> ... 187 more files.

Nothing says the second value is the tech this one leads to. I would copy the wrong string, or think
the tag can hold two ids.

*Fix:* explain the second `<UID>` and quote the nested line so the difference is visible.

### F6 — Major: limits are mostly absent

Only a handful of fields carry an "Allowed range" line, while 511 numeric tags have none. Before,
`MaxOpponents` had no minimum, maximum or behaviour at 0. `StartingPlanetRichnessBonus` said "1
means one richness step above the roll" without saying what the scale is or which planets get it.
For a modder the limit is the difference between a balance change and a broken game.

*Fix:* add the line where it is known, and keep the count visible. This pass added six; 511 remain,
which is the largest single gap in the document.

### F7 — Major: units and targets are ambiguous

`StartingPlanetRichnessBonus` said "an empire's capital", but the code adds the bonus to every
planet the empire owns at generation. The values alone cannot settle that; the card asserted the
narrower reading. `Thrust` says "sublight acceleration" without saying it is a force divided by
mass, so I cannot predict what doubling it does.

*Fix:* name the unit and the exact target of the value, and correct claims the code contradicts.

### F8 — Major: the template over-fires on assets

`Textures/.png` is good: replace by relative path, missing image draws a red placeholder. But `.wav`,
`.fbx`, fonts and particles get a `values only` badge because they have no level-3 formula, which is
not a defect: for an asset the useful facts are path, format and failure mode. The index count
includes roughly a hundred cards of this kind, which makes the coverage number look worse than the
reader's experience.

*Fix:* exempt asset families from the field-card contract, or give them a separate "asset card"
shape.

### F9 — Minor: how to see the change in game

Section prose says starting values are baked into a save and XML edits need a restart, but individual
cards rarely say where to look. For `MaxOpponents` I had to infer "new game, setup screen" from the
name. A one-clause "check it here" would remove the guess.

### F10 — Minor: the loader is on the family, not the card

The family line names the loader class; the card does not name the field. At level 3 I sometimes
wanted the exact symbol to search the source later. This matters less for a pure modder and more for
someone moving from modding to code.

## Author response

The author read this review with the code open. Changes were made in the generator and the prose,
then the pages were rebuilt and re-audited.

### W1 — Fixed: one example for every format (F4)

`stat_line` now takes the family's format and quotes the line in the syntax of the file it came
from: `<Tag>value</Tag>` for XML, `key=value` for `.hull` and `.design`, `key: value` for YAML. The
count is the number of other sampled files.

> In vanilla: `Hulls/Cordrazine/Cordrazine_Station.hull` — `HullName=Cordrazine/Cordrazine_Station`;
> the other 124 files set their own.

> In vanilla: `Hulls/Cordrazine/Cordrazine_Station.hull` — `Version=1`; all 125 files set it the
> same way.

Result: 329 collapsed lines across the pages, up from 260. The hub and the explainer prompt now state
the rule for all four formats, not just XML.

### W2 — Fixed: no restated value as level 3 (F1, F3)

A level 3 that is only `Vanilla 7.` or `1.` is no longer rendered as detail. Cards without prose
level 3 get a `values only` badge, a heading that says "the values, and what is missing", and this
sentence:

> This card is an index entry: it names the field and quotes the values the content files set, but
> the guide has not yet documented its unit, accepted range or failure symptom. Do not infer a bound
> from the vanilla values.

32 fake level 3s are gone; 970 cards now say what they are. The audit reports the count so the gap
cannot silently grow or be mistaken for coverage.

### W3 — Fixed: the document audits its own conformance (F2, F6)

`build-moddable.py --audit` now prints, next to the sentence-cap checks:

```
conformance: prose level 3 95/1065; level 2 under two sentences 1049/1065;
numeric tags with no Allowed range 511
index-only cards (no prose level 3): 970
numeric tags with no Allowed range: 511
```

The last two lists name cards, so the next author pass can work down them instead of guessing where
the gaps are.

### W4 — Fixed: eleven cards got real detail (F5, F6, F7)

| card | what it now answers |
|---|---|
| `Globals/MaxOpponents` | cycles 1..value, corners uses 3, 0 collapses to 1, no clamp |
| `Globals/DefaultNumOpponents` | opening count, not a cap; expected 1..MaxOpponents |
| `Globals/StartingPlanetRichnessBonus` | added to every owned planet, slider 0..5, richness step math |
| `Globals/ShipDestroyThreshold` | internal-slot share, 0/0.5/1 outcomes, retreat interaction |
| `Globals/ShieldPowerMultiplier` | applied via `EmpireHullBonuses`, 0.5/1/2 math, negative caveat |
| `Technology/UID` | own id vs child `<UID>` inside `LeadsTo`, with the nested example |
| `Technology/MaxLevel` | repeat research, 1.4 multiplier, 0 makes it unpurchasable |
| `Modules/UID` | overwritten from the stem; editing the tag changes nothing |
| `Weapons/UID` | stem is the join key; a wrong `WeaponType` leaves the mount inert |
| `Buildings/Name` | tech join key, five names code looks up, silent unbuildable |
| `Races/StartingScout` | design name, per-race example, default fallback |

Six of them also carry an Allowed-range line with the arithmetic at each bound. That leaves 511
numeric fields without one; ships, globals and buildings are the order to take them in.

### W5 — Fixed: the rule is in the generator and the prompt

The hub's "two-pass depth" section now explains the one-file example for hulls as well as races, and
the `values only` marker. The explainer prompt's answer shape and checklist say to quote a per-file
tag in its own syntax, and that a restated value such as "Vanilla 7." is not a level 3.

### W6 — Deferred, with the reason

- **Level 2 on 1,049 cards** is a writing job, not a rule.  A generated second sentence ("Read by the
  loader") would be filler, which is what this review objects to. The 11 cards changed here got a
  written level 2 (eight of them two sentences); the rest need one written per field.
- **511 numeric fields without bounds** need the consuming code read one at a time. The audit lists
  them; inventing a bound from the vanilla values is exactly the failure this pass removed.
- **Asset cards** still get the field-card marker. Splitting the renderer into an asset shape is a
  template change, not a content fix; it is the cheapest next improvement to the coverage number.
- **Level 2 for assets** is already adequate in the family prose; the card shape, not the content,
  is what needs the exception.

## What the tester still says

The top of the document is now trustworthy: I can tell a documented card from an index card, I get a
usable example for per-file tags in every format, and the globals I tried first have limits and
arithmetic. The middle is still a catalog: `Thrust`, `Cost`, `Traits`, most weapons and most map
tables remain values without a unit or a failure mode. I would still open the source for those, and
the page now tells me I have to.

That is a pass on honesty and a partial pass on usefulness. The next win is not another template: it
is writing the level 2 paragraph and the range line for the 30 fields a first ship, colony or map
mod touches, then letting the audit count fall.

## Limits of this review

- The tester never ran the game. Every claim about play comes from the page and the values, not from
  a loaded save.
- The author checked the eleven documented cards against the code; the remaining cards were not
  re-verified.
- Coverage numbers are the generator's audit at `ef406fa`; they move whenever content or prose is
  edited.
- No commit was made. The review, the generator changes and the regenerated pages are uncommitted.
