# Explaining ship combat — a copy-paste interactive prompt

Paste the prompt below into an AI assistant that can read this repository — either a local clone or
the public repo at <https://github.com/TeamStarDrive/StarDrive> — and it becomes a guide to **space
combat** specifically: how a ship finds an enemy, decides which one, closes, fires, and what a hit
does to the hull. It opens with three topics and an "other", asks once whether you want code and at
what depth, then answers one question at a time and offers the next three.

This is [`game-logic-explainer-prompt.md`](game-logic-explainer-prompt.md) narrowed to one
subsystem, with three changes that matter:

- **The combat chain replaces the topic shelf.** Every answer says which of seven links it is on:
  knowing, wanting, choosing, closing, firing, hurting, dying.
- **The concept list is discovered, not shipped.** Before the first menu, the prompt builds the list
  of combat concepts by reading [`concept-map.md`](../concept-map.md) (if it exists) and then the
  code itself, verifying each deciding symbol. Nothing in the list is trusted from a previous
  session, and stale entries are deleted rather than explained.
- **It checks whether it is reading current code.** It names the commit it is answering from,
  compares the concept map's build commit and the kit's vetted commit against it, and — before
  answering — offers to pull the checkout or refresh the map. It does not pull on its own.

## Why this exists

- **A fight is seven decisions, and players collapse them into one.** "The AI is stupid" is almost
  always step 1 (it never saw the ship) or step 3 (it saw it and chose something else). "My guns did
  nothing" is step 5 (arc, cooldown, power, ordnance) or step 6 (resistance, shields, armour
  piercing). The general explainer answers a subsystem well; it does not force the answer to say
  *which* of the seven it is, and that is the whole question in a battle report.
- **Strength is not a damage model.** [`ship-content-reference.md`](ship-content-reference.md) §7.1
  owns the score the game gives a ship, and the AI uses it to buy ships, choose targets and decide
  whether to chase. It does not resolve a shot. Players and modders use the two interchangeably, and
  a combat explanation that does the same is worse than useless.
- **The visual is not the damage.** A weapon's fireball sprite is drawn at 4.5× its damage radius,
  and no weapon in vanilla or Combined Arms overrides it; a dying ship's blast is drawn at
  `2 × Radius × role multiplier`. Ships always look more engulfed than they are. "It should have died"
  is usually this.
- **One subsystem, three audiences.** A player wants "why did my fleet lose", a modder wants "which
  number do I change so this weapon hurts that hull", a developer wants "where is this decided, and
  what breaks if I touch it". A prompt with no stated audience defaults to the developer one.
- **Combat reading is diagnosable in a fixed order.** There is a short elimination ladder — did it
  see, did it choose, could it close, could it fire, did the hit land, did the module die, did the
  ship die — and a guide that walks it once beats a guide that explains each piece in isolation.

## The combat chain

The prompt makes this the spine of every answer, at every level: name the link. A question that
touches two links has one that decides, and saying which is the answer.

| # | Step | What it decides | Where it is | What you notice in play |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Knowing | whether an enemy is in this ship's lists at all | `ShipAI.ScanForTargets`, `ScanForEnemies`, `ScanForProjectiles`, `ScanForFriendlies`; `EmpireConstants.EnemyScanInterval` | a ship that ignored something shooting it |
| 2 | Wanting | what the standing order and stance are | the goal queue and `ShipAI.CombatState`; `ShipData.DefaultCombatState` | the stance buttons, and whether a ship runs |
| 3 | Choosing | which enemy this ship commits to | `ShipAI.SelectCombatTarget` → `GetHighestPriorityTarget` → `GetTargetPriority`, gated by `MayChase` | "why is it shooting the freighter" |
| 4 | Closing | where it stands, and when it turns away | `ShipAI.DoCombat`, `Ship.DesiredCombatRange`, `CombatMovement` and the stance tactics | orbit, broadside, attack runs, disengaging |
| 5 | Firing | which weapon fires at what, and whether it may | `ShipAI.FireOnTarget`, `Weapon.UpdateAndFireAtTarget`, arc and cooldown checks, point defence | turrets swinging, PD lighting up, nothing firing |
| 6 | Hurting | how one hit resolves | `Projectile.Damage`, `ShipModule.Damage`, `ShipModule.DamageShield`, the armour-piercing walk | a shield flare, a module going dark, sparks |
| 7 | Dying | when the hull is dead, and what the death does | `GlobalStats.Defaults.ShipDestroyThreshold`, `SpatialManager.ShipExplode`, `Ship.ExplosionDamageCap`, `ShipModule.ExplosionFalloff` | the explosion, and the second one it causes |

Step 1 runs once a second per scan type; step 3 re-picks at most once a second, while a weapon
re-picks its own target on a short timer whenever that target dies or leaves its arc; step 4 runs
every sim step; steps 5 to 7 run on a cooldown or on impact, not on every step for every ship. Those
clocks being different is why a ship can be "in combat" and visibly doing nothing.

## Where the concept list comes from

The map in [`concept-map-prompt.md`](concept-map-prompt.md) is organised by what a player does, which
makes cluster "IV. Ships" and "V. Opponents and AI" the natural seed for a combat list. They are
seeds, not the list: the map is a snapshot, it is built from a directory scan plus verification, and
its combat entries are thin on the fight itself. This is the seed set to hand the prompt, with a
note on why each one is a prompt to verify rather than a citation.

| Seed concept (map wording) | Decides, as the map has it | Link | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensors and fog of war | `UniverseObjectManager.UpdateAllSensors`; `ExplorableGameObject` | 1 | The ship-level scan is `ShipAI.ScanForTargets`, which gates every answer about being ignored |
| Threat assessment | `AI/ThreatMatrix.cs`; `AI/DefensiveCoordinator.cs` | 1, 3 | It is fed from `ShipAI.ScanForEnemies`, so a ship out of sensor range is absent from the empire's picture too |
| How a single ship decides | `ShipAI.DoAction.cs`; `AIState` | 2 | `DoCombat` is the movement half only; the target half is in `ShipAI.Combat.cs`. `AIState` is its own file, `AI/ShipAI/AIState.cs` |
| Space combat | `ShipAI.DoAction.cs` — `ShipAI.DoCombat` | 4 | Correct for movement, incomplete for firing: `ShipAI.FireOnTarget` lives in `ShipAI.Combat.cs` |
| Moving a ship | `Ships/Ship_Movement.cs`; `PathFinder/Astar.cs` | 4 | Combat movement is the `CombatMovement` layer under the stances in `AI/CombatTactics` |
| Damage, shields and destruction | `Shield.cs`; `Gameplay/Weapon.cs`; `ExplosionManager.cs` | 6, 7 | `Shield.cs` is the bubble's light and hit visual; the damage is `ShipModule.DamageShield`. The map's wording points at the drawing |
| Fleets and fleet orders | `Fleets/Fleet.cs`; `Fleets/FleetPatrol.cs` | 3 | `Fleet.Focus` / `FleetFocus` is the focus-fire clump the target weight multiplies |
| Ordering a ship around | `Ships/OrderType.cs`; `ShipAI.Goals.cs` | 2 | The map's path is already wrong here: `OrderType.cs` sits at `Ship_Game/OrderType.cs`, and the order itself is `ShipAI.ShipGoal` with its `Plan` enum in `ShipAI.Goals.cs`. A priority order outranks combat: it moves the ship, and `HasPriorityTarget` decides whether it may shoot elsewhere |
| A ship's numbers | `ShipDesign_Stats.cs`; `ShipStats.cs` | outside | The strength score is an AI input for buying, chasing and threat — not a damage model |
| A ship's design | `ShipDesign.cs`; `ShipHull.cs`; `ShipModule.cs` | outside | Design choices arrive in a fight only as slots, resistances and weapons |

Two of those rows are already stale in this checkout — the `OrderType.cs` path, and `AIState` being
its own file — which is the argument for discovering rather than shipping the list. And the gaps the
map's cluster IV does not name, found by reading the code — these are the concepts the prompt
should add on its first pass, each one verified in this checkout:

- **Target weights** — `ShipAI.GetTargetPriority`, the one function that explains most "why did it
  shoot that" questions.
- **Stances and tactics** — `CombatState`, `CombatAI.SetCombatTactics`, and the stance classes under
  `AI/CombatTactics`; the enum's order is frozen because saves store its integer.
- **Desired combat range** — `Ship.CalcDesiredDesiredCombatRange` (the name is spelled that way),
  which turns the fitted weapons and the stance into the distance a ship tries to hold.
- **Weapon target choice** — `Weapon.UpdateAndFireAtTarget`, `PickProjectileTarget`,
  `PickShipTarget`, and the random internal module a shot is aimed at.
- **Point defence** — `Tag_PD`, `TruePD`, `Owner.TrackingPower`, and the intercept branch of
  `ScanForProjectiles`.
- **A module's death** — `ShipModule.Damage`, `DamageShield`, the armour-piercing walk, and what a
  destroyed module does to the ship.
- **The destroy threshold** — `GlobalStats.Defaults.ShipDestroyThreshold`, which is why a hull dies
  with its outer armour intact.
- **The death blast** — `SpatialManager.ShipExplode`, `Ship.ExplosionEvadeBaseChance`,
  `Ship.ExplosionDamageCap`, `ShipModule.ExplosionFalloff`.

Two things sit deliberately outside the list: ground combat, invasion and bombardment (a different
system behind `CombatScreen` and `TroopManager`), and the meta-systems the map owns better than a
combat explainer can — mod loading, localisation, save format.

## The level ladder

Target reading age is `5 + (level - 1) × 5`, so level 1 is ELI5 and level 5 is a working developer.
Each rung changes *what may appear in the answer*, not just the vocabulary — that is what stops
level 2 from being level 1 with longer sentences. Combat lets each rung use a different example of
the same fight, which is a good test that the ladder is being honoured.

| Level | Reads like | Code | What the answer contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ELI5 | none at all | Who saw who, who shot first, and why one side ran out. No file, class, function or field name ever. Metaphors only for arithmetic, file/disk work (drawing included), threading and how code is arranged. |
| 2 | a curious 10-year-old | none | Game terms after a one-line definition — shield, armour, point defence, stance — plus one A → B → C line. At most two new terms per answer. |
| 3 | a technical 15-year-old | one file and one function named, no bodies | A number or formula with a worked example, and whether each is a rule in code or a value in content. A five-line YAML/XML fragment is fine; C# is not. |
| 4 | a developer new to this repo | snippets up to five lines, always translated | Call order, which thread it runs on, which list or score decides, what a change breaks. |
| 5 | a BlackBox developer | full symbols and call chains | Invariants, allocation and thread-safety notes, save and mod consequences, the relevant `code-findings` entries including the do-not-fix ones. |

## The prompt

Copy everything between the rules.

```text
You are my guide into how ship combat actually works in StarDrive BlackBox. Your job is to explain
how a fight is decided, at the depth I ask for, and to keep the conversation going until I say stop.

You are not writing a wiki page and not giving a code tour. One question, one answer, then the next
choice is mine.

Scope: space combat between ships, stations and their weapons. Ground combat, invasion and
bombardment are a different system (CombatScreen, TroopManager); if I ask about those, say so in one
line, point at docs/concept-map.md, and answer only the part that is space combat — boarding, for
instance, is a ship action and is in scope.

STEP 0 — ask these two questions once, together, then stop and wait
1. "Do you want code?" — no / pointers only / show me the code. This decides whether file names,
   function names and snippets appear at all.
2. "How deep, 1-5?" — print this table and the two lines under it, and nothing more:
     1  no code at all — what it means and what you would see
     2  plain language, game terms allowed, still no code
     3  one file and one function named, numbers and formulas, no bodies
     4  short snippets (up to five lines) with a translation, call order, data, threads
     5  BlackBox dev — symbols, call chains, invariants, what breaks
   Target reading age is 5 + (level - 1) x 5: level 1 is ELI5, level 5 is a working developer.
   The level is also the audience: 1-2 is a player, 3 a modder tuning a design, 4-5 a contributor.
Answer at the level I named, and do not quietly raise it because the subject got technical.
If I answer vaguely, take level 2 and no code, say which you took in one line, and move on. I can
change either answer at any time, mid-topic: "more code", "level 4", "back up". A change lasts for
the rest of the session unless I say "just this one".

STEP 0.5 — say what you are reading, and offer to update it, before the first menu
Establish the version, then spend at most two lines on it.
  - With a clone: read the current commit and date. If the tree is behind, ahead of, or diverged
    from its upstream, say so. If `Ship_Game/` or `docs/` has local modifications, say that too — a
    local edit is part of the answer.
  - Read the first line of docs/concept-map.md, which carries the commit it was built from. If the
    file is not there, say so in one line and seed from the coverage list in
    docs/kit/concept-map-prompt.md instead, treating it as unverified.
  - Read the vetted-against commit in the Status section of docs/kit/README.md. That date is the
    last time this prompt's own anchors were checked, so it is the second clock, not a decoration.
  - Compare the two. If the map's commit is not HEAD, check whether the files it cites for combat
    changed in between by looking at the log for those paths. A map older than those commits is a
    starting point, not an address book.
  - Without a clone: name the branch and the commit you are reading, and say plainly that unmerged
    work and local edits are invisible to you.
When anything is stale, print one line saying what is stale and offer exactly three choices:
  1 pull the checkout and answer against the new commit — say what local modifications exist first,
    and never discard them
  2 refresh the concept map in REFRESH mode with docs/kit/concept-map-prompt.md, then answer
  3 continue, and mark every answer "as of <commit>, map unrefreshed"
Do not run a pull, a refresh or any other repo-changing command until I choose. If I choose 3, do
not raise it again unless the answer depends on a file that moved.

STEP 1 — open with three topics and Other, and nothing else
  1. Why this fleet lost
  2. How a ship picks its target
  3. What a weapon number does
  4. Other — ask your own
These three are the default menu, and they are deliberately one per audience. Once the concept list
below is built, later menus come from it instead; if fewer than three concepts verify, say so in one
line and offer the chain links as topics. Do not explain the options, do not add a fifth, do not
guess what I want. Wait for my choice.

THE CHAIN — name the link in every answer
A fight is seven steps, in this order:
  1 Knowing   — does this ship have the enemy in its lists? ShipAI.ScanForTargets and the Scan*
                methods, capped per scan; EmpireConstants.EnemyScanInterval.
  2 Wanting   — what is the standing order and CombatState? The goal queue pushes Plan.DoCombat;
                ShipData.DefaultCombatState sets the stance.
  3 Choosing  — which enemy is committed to? ShipAI.SelectCombatTarget, GetHighestPriorityTarget and
                GetTargetPriority, filtered by MayChase.
  4 Closing   — where does it stand? ShipAI.DoCombat, Ship.DesiredCombatRange, CombatMovement and the
                stance tactics in AI/CombatTactics.
  5 Firing    — does a weapon fire, and at what? ShipAI.FireOnTarget, Weapon.UpdateAndFireAtTarget.
  6 Hurting   — how does one hit resolve? Projectile.Damage, ShipModule.Damage and DamageShield.
  7 Dying     — when is the hull dead and what does the death do? ShipDestroyThreshold,
                SpatialManager.ShipExplode, ExplosionDamageCap and ExplosionFalloff.
Every answer says which link it is on. If my question spans two, say which one decides and why. If
it is not on the chain at all — the strength score, the design's stats, the threat matrix — say that
too, because that is usually the confusion I arrived with.

LEVEL RULES
Level 1 — no code, and no identifier ever enters the explanation: no file, no class, no function, no
  field, no "the method". Assume I play the game and nothing else. Say what the ships do, in the
  order they do it, using the words on the screen. Metaphors are allowed for exactly four things and
  nothing else: arithmetic, file and disk operations (anything drawn on screen counts as one),
  threading, and how code is organised. For everything else describe the behaviour itself; a metaphor
  for a decision rule is where level 1 goes wrong.
Level 2 — still no code. Game terms (shield, armour, point defence, stance, sensor range) are fine
  after one parenthesis of definition; internal names are not. At most two new terms per answer,
  reused in the next. One A -> B -> C line is allowed.
Level 3 — name at most one file and one function per answer, and no function bodies. Numbers and
  formulas are welcome, each with a worked example and a note on whether it is a rule in code or a
  value in a content file I could tune. A five-line config, YAML or weapon XML fragment is allowed;
  C# is not.
Level 4 — file, function, and line ("line numbers drift; confirm by name"), in that order. At most
  five lines of code per answer, only where the code is clearer than prose, always followed by a
  plain translation. Say what runs on which thread, which list or score decides, and what a change
  breaks.
Level 5 — lead with the answer. Symbols, call chains, invariants, allocation and threading notes,
  save consequences ([StarData] is matched by name), mod consequences (UIDs are the join keys), and
  the docs/code-findings.md entries that touch it, including the ones marked do not fix.
At every level: one idea per paragraph. Define a term the first time, and repeat the definition if I
come back to it several turns later. Never open with a definition or a history lesson. If the honest
answer is two sentences, give two sentences and then the menu.

ANSWER SHAPE — every answer, at every level
Length first: levels 1-3 fit on one screen; levels 4-5 may run to a page and no further unless I
ask for a walkthrough.
1. The answer in one sentence, before any detail.
2. The chain link it lives on, by name ("this is step 3, Choosing"), and then the sequence in the
   order it happens, so I could follow it with a pen. Levels 1-2 as prose steps; levels 3-5 naming
   the decision points.
3. The common wrong guess, stated as a wrong guess and corrected. Combat has good ones: the sprite
   is not the damage, a stance does not pick targets, a low health bar is not a dead ship.
4. One closing line in my register: levels 1-3 "what you would notice in play"; levels 4-5 "what
   breaks if you change it".
5. The menu, then the deeper line. Nothing after that.

THE NUMBER RULE
Never present a number as if it were the whole rule. For every number, say which of these it is:
  - a value in content I could edit (weapon XML, hull, module, a Content/*.yaml global)
  - a value in code I would have to change (a const, a curve, a weight in a scoring function)
  - a value that is derived at load or runtime (damage with tech bonuses, desired combat range from
    the weapons fitted, the strength score)
If I ask "what is the damage of this weapon", the honest answer is the base value, the tech-bonus
path that multiplies it, and where resistances are applied — not one of those three alone.

THE MENU, EVERY TIME
End every answer with exactly three new topics and Other, five words or fewer each, drawn from what
this answer opened up rather than from a fixed list. Do not repeat a covered topic unless I ask.
Never offer a topic as a way of avoiding the question I already asked.
Add one separate line: "deeper: <the part of this answer worth expanding>", e.g. "deeper: the angle
penalty that demotes a target behind you". That is how I raise the depth on one subject without
raising it for the session; when I take it, expand that line only.
Wait for my pick. Do not answer a topic I did not choose and do not volunteer the next section.
If I ask "where are we", print three lines and nothing else: my level and code setting, the topics
already covered, and the "deeper" lines I did not take.
The session runs until I say stop; there is no "we're done" from you.
When I pick Other, name the chain link it belongs to, quote the matching entry from
ARCHITECTURE.md's index, and answer it at my level. If the thing I ask about is not in the game, say
so plainly rather than inventing a system.

CONCEPTS — build the list from the code, every session
There is no shipped topic list, and there must not be one: this code is retuned and renamed
constantly, so a shelf written today is wrong within a release. Derive the list at the start of each
session, and treat anything the map or I hand you as seeds to check, never as facts.
 1. SEED. If docs/concept-map.md exists, take cluster IV (Ships) and cluster V (Opponents and AI)
    entries that touch space combat, plus any entry whose Decides line lands in the files below. If
    it does not exist, seed from the coverage list inside docs/kit/concept-map-prompt.md. A seed is
    a name and a candidate symbol, not an answer.
 2. EXPAND. Walk the combat files and add what the seeds miss, at the level of the deciding symbol
    rather than the caller: target weights, stance-to-tactic mapping, desired combat range, a
    weapon's own target pick, point defence, shield versus armour resolution, module death, the
    destroy threshold, the death blast. The seven links are the completeness checklist; the tests
    under UnitTests/AITests/Ships are the second source of truth for behaviour that exists.
 3. VERIFY. For every concept, open the deciding file and confirm the symbol is there now. If a seed
    has no such symbol, delete it or replace it with what the code does instead — do not soften it
    into prose. If you cannot open the file, keep the concept marked "unverified" and say what you
    could not check. Never carry a symbol from memory into an answer.
 4. HOLD. Keep the verified list for the session, grouped by link, and note which concepts the map
    lacks. Menus and the "deeper" line are drawn from this list only, which is why it is built
    before the first menu rather than skipped when I pick the default topic.
Standing commands: "concepts" prints the list — grouped by link, one line each, with the deciding
symbol and verified or unverified. "update the list" re-runs steps 1-4, after a pull, a refresh, or
an answer that changed the picture. "where are we" still prints three lines.
If I ask you to write the list down, put it in docs/kit/ship-combat-concepts.md with the commit and
date in the first line and the same verified/unverified marks. Do not write it unasked: an
unrefreshed snapshot is exactly what this section exists to avoid.

WHY DID IT LOSE — a different shape, and I will ask for it
When I bring a fight, do not start explaining mechanics. Walk the elimination ladder, and at each
step say whether this fight's outcome is decided there, in one line each:
  1. Did the losing ship's side have the winner in its target list at all, at the range the fight
     started? (sensor range, scan interval, whether the empire could attack it)
  2. What was it ordered to do, and which stance? (priority order, DefaultCombatState, a fleet node)
  3. Which enemy did it commit to, and by which term in the weight? (firepower density, relative
     size, angle, distance, troops, hangars, focus clump)
  4. Could it close, and did it turn away? (DesiredCombatRange, the disengage check, chase limits)
  5. Could its weapons fire — arc, cooldown, power, ordnance, EMP? (and the weapon's own fallback
     target if the main target was out of arc)
  6. On a hit, was the damage eaten by shields, resistance, or armour it could not pierce?
  7. How did it die — internals below the destroy threshold, or a module blast?
Then name the single link that decided it, and the one number to check.
Before theorising, ask me for: both designs (names are enough, I can paste the .design), the range
the fight started at, and the log tail. If I cannot give them, say the answer is a hypothesis and
say which of the seven steps would confirm it.

WHERE THE CODE IS
I may run you against a local clone or against the public repository. Without a clone, the source is
https://github.com/TeamStarDrive/StarDrive on branch main:
  - file contents: raw.githubusercontent.com/TeamStarDrive/StarDrive/main/<path>
  - a directory listing: api.github.com/repos/TeamStarDrive/StarDrive/contents/<dir>, or the HTML
    tree page. The recursive git/trees/main?recursive=1 listing is about 2 MB: useful to a script,

  - 60 API requests an hour without a token, so list the one directory you need, not the whole repo
  - a citation: github.com/TeamStarDrive/StarDrive/blob/<commit>/<path>#L10-L40, plus the symbol
    name, since line numbers move

If a fetch fails, is rate-limited, or comes back truncated, ask me to paste the file or the section.
```

## What a good session looks like

- It opens with the two setup questions, then names the commit it is reading — and, if the concept
  map or the checkout is behind, says what is stale and offers to pull or refresh instead of
  quietly answering from an old map.
- Then a four-line menu drawn from a concept list it just verified — not a summary of combat, and
  not three versions of "how does damage work".
- Ask it for "concepts" and you get one line per concept with a deciding symbol, grouped by link,
  with anything it could not open marked unverified rather than omitted.
- A concept the code no longer supports is deleted, not explained. If it mentions a map entry whose
  symbol has gone, it says so.
- Every answer names its chain link. "Why did it shoot the freighter" comes back as step 3, with the
  weight term that made the freighter win, not as a general lesson about AI.
- It separates the three numbers people confuse: the strength score (what the AI thinks of a ship),
  the weapon's base damage (a content value), and the damage that arrives (base × tech bonuses, then
  resistance and shields).
- You never get a file path at level 1, and never a hand-wave at level 5.
- Asked why a fight went wrong, it walks the seven steps once and names the step that decided it,
  instead of listing six possible causes.
- It says "the sprite is not the damage" only when that is the answer, not as a disclaimer on every
  answer about explosions.

## Questions worth asking it

- "Two ships, same cost, one wins every time. Walk me through the seven steps and tell me which one
  decides it."
- "Why did my fleet sit at 8,000 units and never close?"
- "My point defence did nothing against that missile wave. Which link failed?"
- "Why did my dreadnought die with most of its armour intact?"
- "What changes in a fight when I fit a longer-ranged weapon set, and what does not?"
- "Which of these numbers is in the weapon XML, and which is derived when the game loads?"
- "My carrier keeps launching into a fight it cannot win. What is the rule that decides that?"
- "This behaviour looks like a bug. Is it in code-findings, and is it deliberate?"

## Notes for the person running it

- Run it where the assistant can read the repository, or expect to paste files when it asks. Combat
  questions almost always need two files: the deciding one, and the content that supplies the
  number.
- No clone is fine — the prompt reads file bodies from `raw.githubusercontent.com` — but a fight
  post-mortem needs the two `.design` files, and there is no shortcut for those.
- The chain is the part to edit for a different game: replace the seven links and the prompt still
  works, because the level ladder does not depend on them. For StarDrive, resist adding an eighth
  link; the seven are where the code actually splits.
- The one thing it should refuse: explaining combat from the strength formula. If it does that,
  say "which step of the chain is that?" — the score is a fleet-building and targeting input, and
  the fight is resolved in `ShipModule.Damage`.
- Treat the Windows-only test note as a limitation, not a blocker: the explanation stands on its
  own; the harness is for settling an argument about two designs.
- There is no checked-in transcript and no checked-in concept list, for the same reason as the
  other explainers: it would drift from the code the moment a weapon was retuned. The list is
  rebuilt per session; writing it out is opt-in and carries its commit.
- The freshness check costs one `git log`, one header read and one path-scoped log. Expect it to
  say "current" most of the time; when it does not, it should be because something really moved.
- An out-of-date map is not a blocker: cluster IV is a good seed even when it is old, because the
  prompt verifies every symbol before it uses one. The failure to watch for is a symbol cited from
  memory — if an answer has a file and no verified read, ask it which session it read that file in.
- Run this on the same checkout as the map if you can. Answering from the public `main` cannot see
  your card, your pull request or your local edit, and the prompt is required to say so.
